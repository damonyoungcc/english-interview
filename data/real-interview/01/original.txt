1. Can you tell me about a situation in your company?
When I joined my previous company, I proactively led the effort to build a shared internal admin system template.
At the time, each team in the company was building their own internal admin systems separately without a shared standard.
As a result, the user experience was really inconsistent and confusing.

2.How did you realize the problem was serious?
The ops team often pointed out that the inconsistent interaction patterns across systems
and made it frustrating to use and slowed them down a lot.
I gradually realized there were more and more issues in our daily collaboration.

3.What steps did you take to understand the root cause of the issue?
First, I took the time to understand everyone’s pain points.
and Then I talked to people from ops, product, and dev teams, and found that a lot of them had the same complaints —
like the navigation was confusing, tables worked differently in each system, and setting permissions was a pain.
In the end I tried to find what everyone really needed in common.

4. What actions did you take to solve the problem?
Then I led the design and development of a shared internal admin system template.
The goal was simple:
One is make it easy for new projects to use with Just one click and immediately focus on business development,
and two is help old ones upgrade with minimal effort.

5.Did you face any challenges or resistance? How did you handle it?
One thing I will say though, is that some teams weren’t that willing to migrate at first.
They had their own priorities and custom setups.
I created a demo, wrote documentation, and provided a migration guide
I try to make the process as easy as possible
so in the end, there was really no excuse not to cooperate.

6. Can you describe a reusable system or template you built?
This project provides two things.
First, a demo with a login page, routing setup, layout, and best practices for tables and forms.
Second, I wrapped components like layout and form based on Ant Design and published them to our internal npm.
So old projects can install these packages step by step to update their UI.
After a few updates, we were able to unify the user experience across all internal admin systems.


7. How do you usually communicate with other teams?
How do you manage communication with different stakeholders?

First thing, I think should try to understand who I’m talking to.
If it’s a product manager, I focus on business impact.
If it’s an engineer, I explain technical details clearly.
I try to speak in their language.

The second I also try to see things from their side.
I ask what’s their pain point, or what they care about the most.
That helps me adjust my message to make it more relevant.
